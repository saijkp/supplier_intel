Why "Established 2008" Means Nothing Until You Check the Registration Date

Supplier Intel

A supplier's "About Us" page says "established 2008" or "18 years of manufacturing experience." It's one of the most common trust signals in a sourcing pitch, and one of the least checked — because checking it takes one extra step most buyers skip: pulling the actual registration date from the registry the claim implies, not the website that makes it.

The check itself is simple

That step is simple where the records are public. In the UK, Companies House shows the real incorporation date for free. For a Chinese entity, the unified social credit code resolves to a registration date through the national registry. Either way, you're comparing two independent timestamps: the age the company claims for itself, and the date a government registry says the legal entity actually came into existence. A cheap, complementary cross-check is the domain's own WHOIS registration date — a company claiming two decades of trading history on a domain registered eight months ago is a pattern worth noticing, even though domain age proves nothing on its own.

A mismatch isn't automatically fraud

Legitimate reasons exist: a sole trader converting to a limited company, a factory re-registering under a new entity after a restructure, a holding company formed years after the operating business it now owns. The point isn't to reject every gap — it's to stop treating "years in business" as self-evidently true just because it's printed on a page the supplier controls. A five-day-old registration behind a claim of five years in business is a different risk profile than a two-year gap with an ordinary corporate-structure explanation, and you can only tell them apart by looking.

Same discipline, different line on the page

This is the same discipline behind every other check we build: don't let a claim stand in for a record. An "established" date is marketing copy until it's checked against the registry that actually tracks when a legal entity started existing — at which point it's either corroborated, or it's a specific, namable question to put to the supplier before you commit. We've written before about why a Companies House shared-address match deserves the same treatment as a registered-office claim; a registration-date check is the same instinct applied to a different line on the same page.
