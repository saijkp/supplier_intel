A BSCI or SMETA "Certificate" Doesn't Exist — Here's What the Audit Report Actually Proves

Supplier Intel

Ask a supplier for proof of ethical compliance and a lot of them will send back something they call a "BSCI certificate" or a "SMETA certificate." Neither exists. BSCI (amfori's Business Social Compliance Initiative) and SMETA (Sedex Members Ethical Trade Audit) don't certify a factory pass/fail — they produce an audit report with a rating, tied to a specific audit date and a specific set of findings. A supplier that uses the word "certificate" for either one is using the wrong term, and it's worth noticing when they do.

That distinction matters because the artifact itself is easy to fake cheaply. The common pattern isn't a from-scratch forgery — it's editing a real report: a genuine PDF from a different, unrelated factory, with the company name, address, and ID number changed before it's sent to you. Basic editing software is enough. The tell isn't always visual. It's whether the IDs on the page resolve to anything.

Check the ID, not the PDF

Both schemes publish the way to check this independently. A BSCI report carries a Business Partner (BP) ID, lookupable on amfori's own platform. A SMETA audit is tied to a Sedex ID (sometimes shown as a ZC code), checkable against Sedex's member database. Either lookup tells you three things the PDF alone can't prove: whether that ID exists at all, whether the registered company name attached to it matches the name on the report you were sent, and whether the audit date is current rather than years expired with no renewal on file.

A gap, not a verdict

A report that doesn't resolve on the issuing platform isn't automatically proof of fraud — IDs get mistyped, access can be restricted by the supplier's own account settings. But it's a specific, closeable gap, not a shrug. The same discipline applies here as with any other registry-backed claim we check: look up the primary source, not the PDF someone emailed you. Evidence-gathered, human-reviewed — a report that resolves and matches is a data point, never a verdict on its own.
