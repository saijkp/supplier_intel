The problem with scores

A "92/100 verified supplier" score tells you nothing about what was actually checked.

Most sourcing platforms compress everything they know about a supplier into a single number or badge. That number can't tell you whether a certification was ever confirmed against a registry, whether anyone actually looked for negative reviews, or whether "verified" meant a site visit or a form the supplier filled in themselves.

Score-based tools

  • One number, no trail. You can't see what pushed the score up or down.
  • Certifications taken at face value. A logo on a page counts the same as a verified registry entry.
  • "Verified" is vague. Usually means a form was submitted, not that anything was independently checked.
  • The platform decides "pass/fail" for you. Your judgment is replaced, not informed.

Supplier Intel

  • Every claim comes with a source URL. Registry pages, the supplier's own site, third-party listings — you can click through to all of it.
  • Certifications are marked checkable or not. A certificate number and issuing body is treated differently from a bare logo.
  • Reputation search is run and reported honestly — including when nothing turns up, rather than treating silence as a good sign.
  • No Pass/Fail is ever written by the system. You make the qualification call, with the evidence in front of you.
How it works

Four steps. None of them a scoring model.

Supplier Intel automates the tedious parts of vetting — finding candidates and gathering evidence — and stops short of the part that should stay human.

1

Discover

Search by product or category to surface real candidate manufacturers, cross-checked against multiple sources rather than a single directory.

2

Collect

The site pulls address, contact details, certification claims, facility photos and factory location directly from the supplier's own pages.

3

Verify

Company registration status, satellite/street-view imagery of the stated factory address, and an independent reputation search are run per candidate.

4

You decide

Every finding is laid out with its source. The qualification decision — pass, flag, or reject — is yours to make and record.
