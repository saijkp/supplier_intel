Why "UK-Registered" Means Nothing Until You Check Who Else Shares the Address

Supplier Intel

A UK company number on a supplier's site looks like proof. It isn't, on its own — and the specific gap worth checking for is one most sourcing consultants don't think to look at: how many other companies are registered at that exact same address.

The check Companies House already lets you do

Companies House's own register is free to search, and it lets you do this directly: look up the registered office address, and see every company filed against it. For a genuine manufacturer, that number is usually small — the business itself, maybe a related holding company. For a registered-office-only presence, it can run into the dozens or hundreds, because company formation agents and virtual-office providers sell the same address to any business that wants a UK registration without a UK footprint.

Why the distinction matters

This matters for the exact reason buyers assume a UK company number means something: it implies accountability, a real point of contact, maybe even UK manufacturing capacity. A company number obtained for a modest formation-agent fee implies none of that. Neither situation is illegal — using a formation agent's address is completely normal for a small or newly-registered business — but it tells you something different about who you're actually dealing with than the supplier's own marketing copy does.

How to run it

The check itself is simple and free: pull the registered office address from the company's Companies House filing, then search that address on the register and count how many unrelated businesses come back.

  • A handful of results — unremarkable. Common for small groups with a holding company structure.
  • A long list sharing SIC codes with company-formation or accountancy services — the tell. It means the address is a service product, not a place where the supplier actually operates.

The wider pattern

A UK company number tells you a legal entity exists, not that it manufactures anything, employs anyone, or occupies the address it filed. Treat it as a starting point for verification, not the verification itself — and check the address the same way you'd check anything else a supplier's site asserts about itself: against the primary source, not the claim.
